# EA App 1.0.145: which build proofs hold

EA App 1.0.145.2472 is `runtime::build_proof::Build::ea_1_0_145`: PE timestamp `1794315435`, SizeOfImage
`0x1615e000`, overlay `re/db/builds/ea-1-0-145.json`. All evidence below is static, from the decrypted dump
`/mnt/gaming/EAApp/ai-fix/dumps/F1_25.1794315435.105156.mem` against the KB exe. RVAs are the KB build's unless they
say "EA".

The dump was taken with the core running. At each point where the core hooks the game, the dump holds the core's
patch (a `jmp` out of the image, then `nop` filler), and the code that was patched over is not in the dump. A function
that holds a patch differs from the KB's from the patch on when the two are compared index by index. Such a function
was compared again with the two instruction streams aligned (difflib on the normalised instructions). It counts as
equal when its only differences are the core's patches and, where this page says so, a shifted displacement.

The signatures were also checked on a restored image: the dump with the core's patches put back from the live run's
`loader.log` ([Signatures](#signatures-on-a-restored-image)).

## Live State

`re/tools/layoutcmp.py --steam <KB exe> --other <dump> --build ea-1-0-145` compares 150 Live State field rows. For 147
of them, the instruction at the KB row's xref has an aligned twin on EA with the same displacement. The other three:

- `CarErs.ers_mode` +0x1a68 is +0x1a6c on EA, as on Steam 1.0.145 (below).
- `GameManagerLive.progress_system` +0x2d0 is +0x2a0 on EA. The game manager's tail sits 0x30 lower, as on the EA App
  KB build (`runtime::game_manager::tail_field`). In 0x1403d84b0 (EA 0x1403d8c30) the 361 instructions are equal
  except six, and each of the six reads a tail field 0x30 lower: +0x2d0 to +0x2a0 three times, +0x2b0 to +0x280,
  +0x288 to +0x258 and +0x2b8 to +0x288. The entrants system moves the same way: in 0x14022d920 (EA 0x14022e0b0) the
  only differences among 1886 instructions are its ten reads, +0x290 on the KB and +0x260 on EA. The overlay's
  distribution is "EA App", so `runtime::manager_entrants_system()` already gives +0x260 there.
- `VehicleTyreComponent.slot_count` +0x9c48: in 0x142ea2a90 the read is under a core patch. The tyre rebuild 0x142e9e6c0
  (EA 0x142e94850) is equal over all 384 instructions, and it stores the count at `slots + 0x78` (+0x9bd0 + 0x78). The
  rest of 0x142ea2a90 is aligned-equal: 216 of 217 instructions, and the one that differs is the patch.

The driver name getter 0x142f28c10 (EA 0x142f1f180) is equal over all 78 instructions. It covers participants
+0x88/+0x90, physics +0x18, car +0x58, the vehicle id +0xe8, the override flag +0x32 and the player name +0x60. The
row's `field_proofs` takes `G_DRIVER_NAMES`, as on Steam 1.0.145. layoutcmp aligns the entrant `driver_ref` +0x50, the
driver table +0x268 and the name +0xc8, and the badge render's `badge_stat_id` +0x68.

The six telemetry builders and the bus functions layoutcmp lists are equal on EA: the car telemetry builder, the lap
data builder, the collision and RTMT messages, the bus setup, `Ne_Subscribe_BusMessage` and the registry lookup. The
car status builder has 1075 of its 1078 instructions identical, and the other three are aligned but differ.

## ERS mode and the engine torque function

The engine torque function 0x1446e2290 (EA 0x1446dad90) has 1509 instructions, and 1498 of them are aligned-equal.
The rest:

- the core's ERS mode guard patch at +0x5df, over `vmovss xmm1, [rdx + rcx*4 + 0x1890]`;
- ten reads of the ERS flag and mode block, each four bytes higher: +0x1a62 to +0x1a66, +0x1a64 to +0x1a68,
  +0x1a65 to +0x1a69 and +0x1a68 to +0x1a6c (`movsxd rax, [r14 + 0x1a6c]`, `cmp dword ptr [r14 + 0x1a6c], 2`).

This is the change Steam 1.0.145 has (re/research/port-steam-26566308.md). The ERS Reset (0x1554791c0) stores the
mode at `[rcx + 0x1a6c]` at the aligned twin of the KB's `[rcx + 0x1a68]` store (layoutcmp). Around the guard's site,
the code before and after the patch is the KB's: the mode loaded from `[r14 + mode]`, then `rdx = (rax << 6) + r14`.
So the guard's registers mean the same thing on EA.

## Season standings

The season loop 0xae9550 (+0x2a8, the entry's season +8; 59 of 59 instructions), the championship 0x7b2140 (+0x1058,
its seasons +0x548 and +0x550; 356 of 356) and the compare 0xb317a0 (161 of 161) are equal index by index.

`S_RECOMPUTE` resolves to the protector thunk (KB 0xb2fa60, EA 0xb2e420). The thunk jumps to the relocated body, KB
0x149618ee0 and EA 0x148fc94a0. Aligned, 127 of the body's 131 instructions are equal to EA's 132. Each use of the
rounds +0x20, +0x28 and +0x40, the 0x20-byte round stride (`add rbx, 0x20`) and the 0x28-byte session stride
(`add r12, 0x28`) is aligned with an equal instruction. The two differences:

- KB `lea rcx, [rsi + 0x10]`. EA loads 0x20000 from a protector global, adds 0xfffe0010 and does
  `lea rcx, [rsi + rdi]`, the same address.
- A `nop` of a different length.

The body's callees, through the aligned calls: KB 0xb30040 (body 0x14962eee0) is EA 0xb2ea00 (0x148fcf770), and KB
0xb30210 (0x14962fff0) is EA 0xb2ebd0 (0x148fd2070).

- 0x14962eee0: 120 of 124 instructions equal. The round stride `add r15, 0x20` (twice) and the reads at +8 are
  aligned. The differences are a branch target, `nop` lengths, and one protector write to its own global on each
  build (`add [rip + ..], ecx` on the KB, `and [rip + ..], 0x9090a20f` on EA).
- 0x14962fff0: 134 of 141 instructions equal (EA 138). The session id reads `[r12 + r14*8 + 0x20]` and
  `[rbp + r14 + 0x20]` and the 8-byte record strides (`sar .., 3`) are aligned. The KB computes 1 as

  KB loads esi from a protector global and adds it to another, and EA adds a protector global to si: rsi is not read
  again on either build. The rest are `nop` lengths.

## Signatures on a restored image

The dump has the core's patches at its hook points. No EA 1.0.145 image without them exists here:

- The installed exe and its copy in `/mnt/gaming/f1-builds/ea-1.0.145.2472` are encrypted on disk. `.arch` (the code)
  and `.xtls` match the dump in 0.4% of their bytes.
- `/mnt/gaming/EAApp/ai-fix/dumps` holds this dump and one of the older EA build.
- The core keeps each patch's original bytes in memory (`PatchSet::original_bytes`) and does not log them. The hook
  trampolines are in the hook pool at base+0x16160000, past the image's end, and are not in the dump.

The run that made the dump logged every scan
(`/mnt/gaming/EAApp/swofty-data/sessions/20261116-235125/game-logs/loader.log`). A line
`scan .arch [PATTERN] -> 1 hit at ... (base+0xRVA)` is the pattern matching the live, decrypted image once at RVA,
so the pattern's fixed bytes were there. `sigverify.py --restore LOG` writes them over the dump in memory. On this
dump it puts back 764 bytes under 120 of the 3657 logged hits. Each of those 120 starts at a `jmp` into the hook pool,
except three: a `jmp` within the image (the LAN tile), a 2-byte `jmp` (the movie tile) and a hit that overlaps one
already put back.

```
re/tools/sigverify.py --ref <KB exe> --port <dump> --build ea-1-0-145 --restore <loader.log>
136 signatures: 136 unique on ref, 135 unique on port, 134 same target, 3 in the protector section (KB build only); 1 failing
```

30 of the 31 Sigs that failed on the dump pass on the restored image. The one that still failed, `S_CHANGED_B`
(`guards/frontend/setup_vm_guard.cpp`), fails because of the game's code. It also matched 0 times in the live run, and
the guard armed 3 of its 4 sites there. Its pattern ends in `cmp byte [rsi + 0x7e6a], 0`, which is
`cmp byte [rsi + 0x7e62], 0` on EA (EA 0xc1e267). Next to `S_VALUE_SET`, the KB's stores to +0x7e69 and +0x7e68 are
+0x7e61 and +0x7e60 on EA, and +0x76d8 and +0x7c80 are the same. The car setup VM is 8 bytes shorter somewhere
between +0x7c80 and +0x7e60. The core reads none of these fields.

The game's own compare reads that byte. The guard doesn't read it; it reads only the car's +0x9f40. `S_CHANGED_B`
now leaves that byte open (`80 BE ?? 7E 00 00 00`), so site B arms on EA 1.0.145. It matches once on
the KB build, Steam 1.0.145 and the restored EA image (EA 0xc1e267). The hooked compare and its `jne` are fixed bytes.
`re/tools/tests/test_setup_vm_sig.py` checks that the pattern matches both builds' bytes and neither of the two sites
that start the same way. sigverify still reports the site as different on EA, because the byte lies within its 0x20
bytes at the hook point.

`VERIFIED_LAYOUT` stays off on EA 1.0.145: one of its Sigs shows a struct that differs from the KB's, and the proof
also sets `SIGNIN_STATE`. The direct start's and the title helper's proofs take EA 1.0.145 on their own
([Direct start and title](#direct-start-and-title)). Its other users now have proofs of their own that name EA 1.0.145:
`CAR_TABLE` (the live car and the postfx rules), `LAP_MODULE_LAYOUT` and `FILL_ENTRANTS`.

## Match hints

The overlay's `hints` give 3686 bindings their match on EA 1.0.145, against 117 before. They come from the restored
image:

```
re/tools/bindverify.py --build ea-1-0-145 --image <dump> --restore <loader.log> --write-hints
coverage: hint 0, scan 3680, anchor 1, unresolved 0, deferred 5, unresolvable 23, total 3709
hints: 3679 binding(s) checked at their match on the image (3569 added, 110 already there), 1 left out, 3685 of 3709 now resolve by hint
```

`--write-hints` scans the whole set and takes the match of each binding that resolves by scan with exactly one hit.
It leaves out a binding that the live run resolved to another target, and it never moves an existing hint. It then
resolves the set again with the hints and restores the file unless every new hint resolves by hint at the scan's
target. The one left out is `Sess_PreRaceToRace_Update`: the live run resolved it to EA 0x3607c90, before its
override, and the image to EA 0x2d7c5a0, which its existing hint already names.

24 bindings don't resolve by hint: the 23 unresolvable ones and `site_FeHome_AfterCareerCall`. Its EA override
(#1265) had the core's own `EB 10` at offset 21, the jump the core writes at EA 0x173eb2c for
`site_FeHome_MovieTileBlock`. The pattern matched the dump only, and 0 times once the restore put back that site's
`41 80 C7 02`. The override is gone, so the site takes its own record again: pattern `45 8D 67 01 4C 89 F1 44 89 E2 E8
?? ?? ?? ?? F6`, anchor 15, expect `F6 46 05 04 74`. This is the record the live run bound, before #1265: one hit at
EA 0x173eb17, `verified=yes`, so the expect held on the clean exe, and installed. On the restored image the pattern
matches once at its hint and the expect fails. The restore puts back only the `F6` of the core's 6-byte hook at
EA 0x173eb26, which reads `F6 82 20 A2 14 90` there. The other expect bytes are under that hook and no log holds them, so
`bindverify.py --restore LOG` takes the live run's `verified=yes` for the same match (EA 0x173eb17, installed at 0x173eb26)
in place of the byte check. With it the EA set resolves 3683 by hint, 13 by scan and 23 unresolvable by design, none unresolved.

## Remaining gaps

What runs on Steam 1.0.145 and not on EA 1.0.145, most used first:

1. `SIGNIN_STATE`, the only user `VERIFIED_LAYOUT` has left. It gates the `SWOFTY_DIRECT_SIGNIN` write into the
   player manager's sign-in state, and its fields were not compared on EA 1.0.145.
2. The test save sandbox (below), built and waiting for its in-game run. Of the live run's 35 unresolved bindings,
   `Ne_SaveSystem2Win_WriteInfoXml` is the only one core code names, and EA 1.0.145 has no such function. The other 34,
   among them Steam workshop, overlay, transport and entitlement code, are named by no core code.

## Test save sandbox

EA 1.0.145 has no `Ne_SaveSystem2Win_WriteInfoXml`, so there is nothing to find. Its save device has no corrupt-save
upload. On the restored dump, slot +0x58 of `vt_Ne_SaveSystem2Win` (EA 0x145b70d50) is 0x144147c50,
`xor eax, eax; ret`. The KB build has `Ne_SaveSystem2Win_WriteInfoXml` (0x144141c00) in that slot. The EA 1.0.145
binding set marks the binding unresolvable for that reason, and the live run logs the reason instead of scanning for
it. On Steam the sandbox wraps that function only to switch the upload off. On EA there is no upload to switch off.

Launcher saves already work on EA 1.0.145. The core wraps `Ne_SaveSystem2Win_Start` and `Ne_SaveSystem2Win_Mount`, and
at Start it points the device's title folder at `saves/<profile>/ea`. Five live runs logged `launcher saves armed`,
with `...\savegame\ea` replaced by `...\saves\default\ea`: sessions 20261116-131853, -133321, -140820, -143017 and
-200539. Each armed 6 to 64 seconds after its wraps installed. In those runs the early stage fires at the game's
`Codemasters F1 25` mutex, when the code is already plain, so the early stage that test runs ask for
(`SWOFTY_EARLY_STAGE=1`) is usable there too.

A sandboxed test run on EA takes the launcher saves' EA backend, pointed at the run folder:

- **Core** (`testing/save_sandbox.cpp`). On the EA distribution, `read_run` takes `Backend::ea_local` with no Steam
  proxy. `arm_ea_local` points the title folder at `<run>/saves/ea` (`run_ea_title_dir`) and logs
  `test sandbox armed (run <id>)`. Mount fails closed as before, and the upload is not wrapped.
- **Save now.** On EA, `save_sandbox_writes()` counts profile saves written in place. A save counts once the files in
  `<run>/saves/ea/profile` change and then stay the same for one second (`SaveSettle`). The agent logs
  `[test] save now: the sandbox served <n> profile save(s) written in the EA title folder`.
- **CLI** (`testsuite/sandbox.rs`). `bindings_for` takes the binding set's `build.distribution`. An EA set needs
  `Ne_SaveSystem2Win_Start`, `Ne_SaveSystem2Win_Mount` and `vt_Ne_SaveSystem2Win`.
- **Run folder.** A sandboxed run folder holds `saves/ea/profile`. `Fixture::install_ea` writes
  `savegame@<save>#<file>` there as `<save>/<file>`. The vault never exchanges Steam and EA saves
  (`Layout::exchanges_with`), so an EA run takes a fixture made from an EA save.
- **After the run.** `sandbox::live_saves` returns the prefix's `AppData/Local/F1 25/savegame/ea/profile` for an EA
  copy. A sandboxed run checks that folder for changes instead of Steam's userdata folder.

`EA_PROVEN` stays false until one sandboxed run on EA 1.0.145 shows three things: `test sandbox armed`, saves under the
run's `saves/ea/profile`, and the player's EA save folder unchanged. Until then the preflight refuses EA career runs.
`fixture capture` takes an EA run's `saves/ea` when the game wrote its saves there (`fixture::capture_ea`). The
fixture keeps each `<save>/<file>` under its Steam name, `savegame@<save>#<file>`, and its `fixture.json` says
`"layout": "ea"`. An EA fixture installs only into an EA run, and a Steam fixture only into a Steam run.
`apps/cli/src/commands/testing/tests/ea_sandbox.rs` runs `open_sandbox`, the install, `close_sandbox` and a capture

## Lap module

The lap host's `build()` bit 1 tells the lap module (private source) that its signatures' code was checked on the
build. Its 59 signatures (`sigverify.py --src` over its sources) and the 24 bindings of its EA 1.0.145 table were
resolved on the restored image: each matches once, and its target is the same code as the KB's (function entries 0x60
bytes alike 1.00, sites 0x20 bytes identical at the hook point).

The 57 functions they sit in were also compared whole, aligned. 49 are equal. In seven, the differences are protector
code: constants that fold to the same values (0x28, 0x50, 0x12, 0x420, 1 and 0, from a global plus or xor an immediate
on one build and an immediate on the other), writes to the protector's own globals, and rip-relative vector loads at a
different displacement.

One function differs in the game's code. 0x1430da5d0 (EA 0x1430d0fc0) builds a 0x30-byte object where the KB and
Steam 1.0.145 build 0x60 bytes with an eight-entry vector at +0x40. Its other instructions are equal (608 of 627),
and the module's two sites in it are in the equal part.
